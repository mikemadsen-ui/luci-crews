## Pain Themes (insurance-specific)

### 1. Agent and broker distribution creates routing complexity
Insurance companies — especially P&C and distribution models — have complex
channel structures. Independent agents, captive agents, broker dealers, and
direct sales teams all need different routing logic.

**Goosehead context:** Goosehead is a franchise insurance distribution model
with hundreds of franchise locations. Routing leads to the right franchise
owner + agent, while tracking performance across a distributed network, was
the core problem LeanData solved at $273K ARR.

**SDR angle:** "How do you currently route leads across your agent/broker
network? How do you make sure the right agent gets the right lead, and how
do you audit it when something goes wrong?"

### 2. Compliance and auditability requirements
Insurance is a regulated industry. Routing decisions need to be auditable —
who got this lead, when, and why. Black-box routing systems create compliance
risk.

**SDR angle:** "One thing insurance customers tell us is that they need to
be able to explain a routing decision after the fact — for compliance,
for disputes, for QA. How are you handling that today?"

### 3. Multi-product, multi-line complexity
Large insurance companies (Unum, Allianz) sell multiple product lines to
different buyer types. A benefits lead routes differently than a P&C lead.
A broker inquiry routes differently than a direct customer inquiry.

**Unum context:** Unum's deal was specifically for Demand Unit / Buying Groups —
routing inbound interest across a complex multi-stakeholder buying process
where group benefits decisions involve HR, Finance, and executive leadership.

**SDR angle:** "For companies selling across multiple lines, the routing
logic gets complicated fast — especially when the same company might be
buying benefits through HR and P&C through a different group. Is that
a problem you're running into?"

### 4. Legacy systems and Salesforce maturity
Many insurance companies are mid-journey in their Salesforce buildouts.
They may have Salesforce but are still routing manually or through
primitive logic.

**SDR angle:** "A lot of insurance companies we talk to are 2–3 years into
their Salesforce implementation and realizing the native routing tools
weren't built for their distribution model. Is that where you are?"

## Competitive Context (insurance-specific)

**Most common competitors in insurance deals:**
1. **Manual routing / internal ops** — Many insurance companies haven't
   formalized routing at all. The competition is spreadsheets and human
   judgment, not a tool.
2. **ZoomInfo/RingLead** — Sometimes bundled into a data contract.
   Weakness: not built for agent/broker distribution models.
3. **Chili Piper** — Less common in insurance (fewer inbound-heavy motions),
   but shows up in insurtech companies with direct sales.
4. **Custom Salesforce flows** — Engineering-heavy insurance companies may
   have built their own. Weakness: unmaintainable as the distribution
   network grows.

**Win angle:** LeanData is the only visual, no-code routing platform built
specifically for complex GTM motions. For insurance, that means it can
model agent/broker hierarchies, territory assignments, and multi-product
routing in one place — without requiring Salesforce engineering for every
change.

### The macro environment (2025-2026)
- Insurance is undergoing a fundamental distribution transformation.
  Traditional agent/broker models are giving way to omnichannel,
  embedded, and API-driven distribution.
- The B2B2C nature of insurance — carriers sell through agents,
  brokers, and embedded partners — creates uniquely complex GTM
  orchestration requirements no standard sales tool was designed for.
- 21 insurtech acquisitions in Q3 2025 alone — most since Q3 2022.
  M&A creates immediate routing and territory complexity.
- US insurance digital ad spending reaching $16.98B in 2026,
  up 12.7% YoY — more digital leads means routing problems compound.

### Key pain points to reference in email copy

1. Franchise and agent distribution routing complexity
   Carriers like Goosehead distribute through thousands of franchise agents.
   Routing logic must account for geography, product line, license,
   and capacity — far more complex than standard B2B lead routing.
   SDR angle: "How do you make sure the right licensed agent gets the
   right lead — and how do you audit it when something goes wrong?"

2. Regulatory audit trail requirements
   State Departments of Insurance require documentation of routing
   decisions: who received a lead, when it was assigned, under what
   criteria, and whether SLAs were met. Manual routing creates no audit log.
   This is intensifying as digital channels generate higher lead volumes.
   SDR angle: "When a regulator asks about a routing decision —
   can you show them the timestamp and the criteria?"

3. Digital transformation handoff gap
   Most carriers have modernized the digital front-end.
   The handoff from digital lead to assigned agent remains manual.
   A prospect submits a form and waits hours or days to be contacted.
   SDR angle: "Your digital intake is fast. What happens to that
   lead in the 2 hours after it's submitted?"

4. Household and policyholder matching across CRM silos
   Same household holds auto, home, life, and commercial policies
   across different systems and reps. No unified account view.
   Cross-sell and renewal signals are invisible across policy lines.
   SDR angle: "When a home policy is expiring next month, does the
   rep handling the auto renewal know about it?"

5. Renewal and expansion motion is manual
   Insurance has predictable revenue cycles — renewal dates are known
   months in advance. Yet most carriers manage renewals in spreadsheets
   with no automated system to detect risk or trigger expansion.
   SDR angle: "What triggers your renewal workflow today — a task
   reminder, or an automated signal based on health indicators?"

6. Buying committee misalignment when buying B2B SaaS
   When insurance companies evaluate GTM tools, the buying committee
   includes IT/Systems, Compliance/Legal, Operations, Distribution,
   and Finance. Without systematic buying group engagement, deals stall.
   SDR angle: "How many stakeholders are typically involved when
   your company evaluates a new GTM system?"
