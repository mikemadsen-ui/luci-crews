## Email Writing Rules — Standing Rules for the Writer Agent
# These apply to ALL verticals and ALL scenarios. Non-negotiable.

### Structure
- Maximum 3 sentences in the body. Not 3 paragraphs. 3 sentences.
- One idea per email. Not a menu of reasons to talk.
- No bullet points in cold outreach — bullets signal a template
- No bold text — signals a formatted template
- No headers or sections — it's an email, not a document
- Total email including signature: under 100 words
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph. Easy to read on mobile.

### Subject line
- 4 words or fewer. Lowercase preferred.
- Good: "routing at [company]" / "quick question, [name]"
- Bad: "Improving Your Revenue Operations Efficiency with LeanData"
- Never use the word "partnership" or "synergy" or "connect"

### Opening line rules
- Never start with "I" — leads to self-centered openers
- Never start with a compliment ("Love what you're building at...")
- Never start with a question as the very first word
- Start with an observation about THEM, not a statement about us
- The observation should be something you could only know
  if you actually looked at their company — not something
  you could say to anyone with their title
- Good: "[Company] scaled to [X] reps last year —"
- Bad: "I wanted to reach out because LeanData helps companies like yours..."

### Phrases that immediately signal AI-generated — never use these
- "I hope this message finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out"
- "I came across your profile"
- "As a [title] at [company], you know that..."
- "In today's fast-paced environment"
- "Leveraging our cutting-edge solution"
- "I'd love to connect"
- "Would you be open to a quick call?"
- "I noticed you recently..." (too obviously scraped)
- "Optimize your [anything]"
- "Streamline your [anything]"
- "World-class" / "cutting-edge" / "next-generation" / "best-in-class"
- Any sentence over 20 words
- Two sentences in a row starting with "I"
- Lists of 3+ things LeanData does
- Explaining what LeanData does in the opening line
- "As we scale..." / "In today's competitive landscape..."

### Personalization rules (source: Lavender benchmark data, 231K emails)
- One specific detail per email — not three
- The detail must imply you understand their SITUATION,
  not just that you found their name and title
- Good: "You're running routing across Chili Piper and Salesforce today"
- Bad: "I saw you're the Director of Revenue Operations at [company]"
- If you have a real signal (tool they use, growth event, recent hire,
  job posting, company announcement): use it as the opening observation
- If you have no signal: use the ICP-level hook for the vertical —
  do not invent fake personalization
- Do not reference LinkedIn directly ("I saw your LinkedIn profile")
- Referencing a blog post, a job posting, a product launch, or a
  tool they use is more credible than referencing their LinkedIn

### Seniority adjustments (source: Lavender benchmark data)
Operations reply rates by seniority, and what works:
- VP/Head level (4.4-4.8% reply rate):
  Lead with a company-level observation tied to a scaling signal.
  Connect to a bottleneck that shows up at their stage.
  Give them a path to delegate. Never feature dump.
  Under 100 words. Direct and efficient.

- Director level (3.4% overall, 8.4% on A-grade emails — biggest lift):
  Get specific about the operational domain they own.
  Name the process, not the category.
  One relevant proof point. CTA frames it as comparing approaches.
  This group is underserved by good outbound — biggest opportunity.

- Manager level (4.3% overall, 6.3% on A-grade emails):
  Focus on the process they run every week and where friction is.
  Structure for mobile — short paragraphs, easy to scan.
  Specific CTA tied to a workflow they own.
  Not "thoughts?" — tell them exactly what the next step is.

- Senior Manager / IC level (5.3% reply rate — highest of all):
  Most casual tone on the ladder.
  Really short. Name the specific task they're spending time on.
  Frame benefit as time saved on that exact task.
  IC rarely has buying authority but has massive influence —
  goal is to start a conversation, not close a deal.

### CTA rules (source: Lavender framework data)
- One CTA per email. Never two options.
- Ask for time, not a demo:
  Good: "Worth 20 minutes?"
  Bad: "Would you be open to a brief introductory call to explore
  whether there might be a mutual fit between our organizations?"
- Never add a calendar link in the first email — that's for after they reply
- Interest-based CTA outperforms direct ask for first touch:
  "Does that sound like where you are?" beats "Can we get 20 minutes?"
- Binary questions (yes/no) drive impulsive replies —
  use for follow-ups and bumps

### The self-check before any email is approved
The QA agent must answer yes to all five before approving:
1. Does the opening reference something specific to this person/company?
2. Is the body under 3 sentences and under 100 words total?
3. Does the email contain any banned phrases from the list above?
   (If yes: rewrite, not patch)
4. Is there exactly one CTA?
5. Would a RevOps leader feel this was written for them,
   or feel like they're on a list?

## Proven Email Frameworks (adapted from Lavender)
# These are the structures that work. Writer agent should pick one per email.
# Do not mix frameworks in one email.

### Framework 1: The Mouse Trap (best for first touch when you have a signal)
Structure: Observation (1 sentence) + Binary question (1 sentence)
The question implies your solution without stating it.
Drives an impulsive reply based on curiosity — priority check.

Example pattern:
"[Company] is [observation about their situation].
Would it be helpful to [implied solution as a question]?"

LeanData application:
"[Company] scaled from [X] to [Y] reps last year.
Would having a visual map of exactly how every lead routes
— and why — help your team keep pace?"

### Framework 2: Mouse Trap + Context (first touch, slightly more detail)
Structure: Observation + Binary question + One sentence on what you do

LeanData application:
"[Company] scaled from [X] to [Y] reps.
Would knowing exactly why every lead routed the way it did
help your team keep pace? LeanData gives RevOps a visual,
no-code routing graph they can own without engineering."

### Framework 3: Vanilla Ice Cream (first touch, most complete)
Structure:
1. Observation (shows you know them)
2. Problem/insight (one sentence, implies pain)
3. Credibility (how you've solved this for others)
4. CTA (interest-based, not a hard ask)

LeanData application:
"[Company] is running routing across [tool] and Salesforce today.
Usually, teams at your stage start hitting edge cases the rules
don't cover — and no one can tell you why a lead went where it went.
[Customer logo] fixed this in one sprint.
Worth seeing how they did it?"

### Framework 4: The Customer Mirror (first touch, story-driven)
Structure:
1. Observation about their company
2. Connect to a similar customer situation
3. What that customer was struggling with
4. Ask if they're seeing the same thing

Works when you have a strong logo story that matches their profile.
No pitch — just a story they can see themselves in.

LeanData application (for fintech):
"Your team is growing fast, [name].
Curious if this sounds familiar — when Ramp was scaling their
RevOps motion, routing rules that worked at 50 reps broke at 150.
No visibility into why a lead went where it went.
Is that a problem you're seeing at [company]?"

### Framework 5: The Thoughtful Bump (follow-up only)
Structure: Context for original outreach + Soft question about feedback
One to three sentences. Shows you've considered their time.

Example:
"Given you're scaling the rev team, thought this was worth discussing.
Did you have any thoughts on my last note?"

### Framework 6: The Breakup (final email in sequence)
Structure:
1. State you've reached out
2. Why you did
3. Assume timing is off
4. Ask them to correct you
5. Clarify it's the last email

Key: "bye for now" not "bye forever." People love to correct you.

Example:
"I've reached out a few times because [company] is [observation].
Thought LeanData could help — going to chalk it up to bad timing.
Is routing something that'll be a priority in the next two quarters?"

## Writer Agent Prompt Engineering Rules
# Source: Synthesized from Lavender and Regie.ai best practices
# These are instructions FOR the writer agent itself — how to think
# about generating email content, not just what to write.

### How to construct the opening observation
The opening observation is the most important line in the email.
It must pass this test: could this sentence ONLY be written about
this specific company, at this specific moment?

Good observation sources (in priority order):
1. A tool they currently use that creates a routing problem
   (e.g., "Running routing across Chili Piper and Salesforce today")
2. A recent growth event — new hires, funding, product launch, expansion
   (e.g., "[Company] added 40 reps in the last 6 months")
3. A job posting that signals a pain point
   (e.g., "Hiring a Revenue Operations Manager right now")
4. A specific company initiative visible from public sources
5. ICP-level hook based on vertical (use only if none of the above available)
   (e.g., "Post-Series B fintech teams usually hit routing complexity
   around the time they're building out their first real RevOps function")

Never use:
- "I saw your LinkedIn profile"
- "I noticed you recently posted about..."
- "I came across your company"
- A generic industry observation with no company-specific anchor

### How to write the problem sentence
- Do not state the problem directly — imply it
- Connect the observation to a challenge the reader already knows exists
- Use tentative language ("I imagine," "Guessing," "Usually teams at your stage")
  — ops and RevOps buyers distrust emails that assume they know the problem
- Good: "Guessing the routing rules are getting more complex to maintain"
- Bad: "Revenue operations leaders like you struggle with lead routing"

### How to write the proof/credibility line
- One customer story, one outcome, stated in plain language
- Use the customer's name (from approved reference list) + one specific result
- Do not say "one of our customers" — name them
- The result should be tied to the same pain as the observation
- Good: "Goosehead Insurance solved this across their franchise network"
- Bad: "We've helped dozens of companies like yours improve their routing"

### How to write the CTA
- One question, answerable with yes or no
- Asks for time, not agreement or interest
- "Worth 20 minutes?" — not "Would you be open to a brief call?"
- "Does that sound like where you are?" — validates the observation,
  invites a reply that gives you something to work with
- Never add a booking link in the first email
- For follow-up emails: even softer — "Did you have thoughts on my last note?"

### Sequence step variation rules
Each step in the Outreach sequence must be meaningfully different.
Not just a different opening line on the same message.

Step 1 (first touch): Lead with the strongest signal or ICP hook.
  Framework: Mouse Trap or Vanilla Ice Cream
  Goal: Get a reply based on curiosity or relevance

Step 2 (first bump, ~3 days): Different angle, same account.
  Framework: Thoughtful Bump or Neutral Insights
  New observation or a third-party resource relevant to their situation.
  Goal: Re-engage without repeating step 1

Step 3 (second bump, ~5-7 days): Reframe the problem differently.
  Framework: Customer Mirror or Clarification
  Introduce a customer story they can see themselves in.
  Goal: Build credibility and create relatability

Step 4 (final, ~10-14 days): Breakup email.
  Framework: The Breakup
  Short, assumes bad timing, asks them to correct you.
  Goal: Get a reply (even a "not now") or a referral to the right person

Rules for ALL steps:
- Each step stands alone — a prospect who reads only step 3
  should still understand why you're reaching out
- Never say "following up on my last email" — just make a new point
- Vary the subject line for every step — not "Re:" chains
- Steps get shorter as the sequence progresses, not longer

### Reading level and sentence construction
- Write at a 6th-8th grade reading level
  (Hemingway App target: Grade 6-8)
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- After every sentence in the email body: skip a line
  (single-sentence paragraphs — easier to read on mobile)
- Vary sentence length: one short, one slightly longer, repeat
- Read the email out loud. If you pause unnaturally, break the sentence.
- If a sentence has a comma in it, consider making it two sentences.

### Salutation rules (from Regie.ai)
- Use "Hey [First name]" for most contacts
- Never "Dear [Name]" — too formal for cold outreach
- Never "Hi [Full name]" — use first name only
- For very senior executives (C-suite): just the first name with no salutation
  is acceptable — skips formality entirely
- Add a line break after the salutation before the body

### Signature rules
- First name only in the sign-off
- Name, title, company on separate lines — nothing else in first email
- No phone number in cold outreach
- No company tagline or value proposition in signature
- No URLs in signature for first email
- One-line social proof below signature is acceptable:
  "We work with Stripe, Ramp, and Brex on this."
- Rotate closing words across sequence steps:
  Step 1: "Best,"
  Step 2: "Thanks,"
  Step 3: "Regards,"
  Step 4: [no closing — breakup emails end abruptly on purpose]

### What the QA agent checks against Regie.ai standards
Before approving any draft:
- Is the email under 90 words total?
- Does every sentence in the body have a line break after it?
- Is the opening observation company-specific (not generic)?
- Is the problem implied, not stated directly?
- Is there exactly one named customer reference (not "customers like you")?
- Is the CTA a single yes/no question?
- Is this sequence step meaningfully different from the previous one?
- Does the salutation use first name only?
- Is the reading level appropriate (no jargon, no long sentences)?

### First Party Data (LeanData's own systems via MCP)

#### Salesforce — Account level
What to pull:
- Account.Type — confirm prospect status (not a customer)
- Open opportunities (IsClosed = false) — skip if any exist
- Account.NumberOfEmployees — company size signal
- Account.Industry — vertical confirmation
- Account.AnnualRevenue — size and segment signal
- Account.BillingCountry / BillingState — geo targeting (EMEA scenario)
- Recent activity on the account (Tasks, Events last 180 days)
- Any prior closed lost opportunities — signals they've evaluated before

How the writer uses it:
- Company size + industry = ICP fit confirmation
- Closed lost history = "you've looked at this before" angle (use carefully)
- Recent activity = check if anyone is already working this account
- If zero activity in 180 days = cold account, no warm angle available

#### Salesforce — Contact level (if contact already exists in SFDC)
What to pull:
- Contact.Title — validate against ICP title parameters
- Contact.Department — confirm RevOps/Sales Ops alignment
- Contact.Email — verify deliverability
- Contact.LastActivityDate — when did we last interact?
- Contact.LeadSource — how did they first engage with LeanData?
- Opportunity contact roles — were they on a prior deal?

How the writer uses it:
- Prior contact role on a closed lost deal = "you've seen LeanData before" angle
- Long time since last activity = re-engagement frame
- LeadSource = web/content = they've researched us; adjust tone accordingly

#### LUCI — Conversation intelligence (luci_search_portfolio)
What to pull:
- Semantic search: "[company name] routing pain" at threshold 0.35
- Semantic search: "[vertical] lead routing problems"
- Filter: data_type_filter = ["customer_voice", "transcription_customer"]
- Filter: speaker_role_filter = "customer"

What signals to look for:
- Tool mentions: "we use Chili Piper / RingLead / Openprise / internal build"
- Pain statements: "leads going cold," "routing errors," "can't audit," "manual process"
- Competitive mentions: any competitor they've evaluated
- Scale signals: "we're growing the team," "adding territories"

How the writer uses it:
- Tool mention = strongest opening hook ("Running routing across [tool] today —")
- Pain statement = use as the implied problem in sentence 2
- Competitive mention = inform which competitive angle to use
- Scale signal = "post-Series B routing complexity" frame
- If no LUCI signals found: fall back to ICP-level hooks from vertical file

#### Avoma — Meeting intelligence (avoma_search_meetings)
What to pull:
- Any past meetings with this account (last 180 days)
- Meeting subjects — what were they about?

What signals to look for:
- Prior LeanData demo or evaluation = they've seen the product before
- Implementation calls = existing customer (should have been caught in SFDC check)
- No meetings found = truly cold account

How the writer uses it:
- Prior evaluation found = "you've looked at this before" is a valid angle
  (use with care — acknowledge it directly rather than pretending it didn't happen)
- No meetings = standard cold outreach, no warm angle

### Third Party Data (ZoomInfo via MCP)

#### ZoomInfo — Company enrichment (zoominfo_enrich_company)
What to pull:
- Employee count and growth rate — is the company scaling?
- Tech stack — what tools do they currently use?
- Funding/revenue data — stage and size signals

- Clearbit or Clay → data enrichment focus = they care about data quality

LOW VALUE (not relevant to routing pain):
- Finance, HR, or product tools
- Infrastructure or engineering tools
- Do not use these in email personalization

How the writer uses it:
- Tech stack hit on routing-adjacent tool = use as opening observation
- Rapid headcount growth = "scaling routing complexity" angle
- Recent funding = "post-funding GTM build-out" angle
- Recent leadership hire (new CRO, new VP RevOps) = "new leader evaluating stack" angle

- Top 1 contact matching ICP title parameters for the vertical
- Contact.Title — validate seniority and function
- Contact.Email — required for enrollment
- Contact.DirectPhone — optional, not used in email
- Contact.LinkedIn — optional reference
- Contact.Seniority — confirm floor (Director+ for fintech, Sr Manager+ for insurance)

Title validation rules:
- Primary: "Revenue Operations" OR "Sales Operations" + VP/Director/Sr Director/Head
- Secondary: "GTM Operations" OR "GTM Systems" + Director/Head/Manager
- Insurance only: "Salesforce" + Head/Senior Manager/Director
- Insurance only: "Distribution Operations" + Director/Manager
- Reject: anything with "analyst" or "coordinator" as primary title
- Reject: "Marketing Operations" as primary (influencer, not decision maker)

Contact selection priority when multiple matches exist:
1. Highest seniority within ICP title match
2. Most recently active in LinkedIn (if available)
3. If tie: prefer Revenue Operations over Sales Operations

## The Governing Hierarchy — Go / No-Go Logic
# Source: ZoomInfo framework, adapted for LeanData AI SDR
# "AI doesn't fail because of the model. It fails because of the data."
# The researcher agent must evaluate all four layers before the writer drafts.
# If any layer fails its check, the record is flagged — not drafted.

### Layer 1: Context (the foundation)
Everything known about the account before outreach begins.
The researcher agent must populate ALL of these before proceeding:

Required context fields:
- Firmographics confirmed (employee count, industry, sub-vertical)
- Account is a genuine prospect (not a customer, not in active pipeline)
- Conversation history checked (LUCI + Avoma — even if empty, must be checked)
- Buying hierarchy understood (who is the economic buyer vs. champion)
  Note: for fintech, VP RevOps is typically economic buyer;
  Director/Sr Director is typically champion. Do not target admin-level
  contacts as primary — they cannot move a deal forward.

If context is incomplete: flag the record, reason = "insufficient account context"

### Layer 2: Timing (reach them when ready)
Stacked signals that indicate NOW is the right time to reach out.
One signal is a reason to reach out. Two or more signals = priority account.

Timing signals to check (in order of strength):

STRONG timing signals (act now):
- New RevOps or Sales Ops leader hired in last 90 days
  (new leaders evaluate the stack — highest urgency)
- Company raised funding in last 6 months
  (post-funding GTM buildout = routing complexity incoming)
- Recent headcount growth 20%+ in sales/marketing roles
  (scaling = routing rules breaking)
- Job posting for Revenue Operations or Sales Operations role
  (actively investing in this function = they feel the pain)
- Company announced expansion into new markets or geographies
  (new territories = routing complexity)

MEDIUM timing signals (good time, not urgent):
- Company launched a new product or product tier in last 90 days
  (new product = new routing logic needed)
- Recent acquisition or merger
  (two Salesforce instances = routing nightmare)
- Tech stack includes routing-adjacent tool (Chili Piper, RingLead)
  (they have the problem — may not know a better solution exists)

WEAK timing signals (proceed but deprioritize):
- General ICP fit with no specific timing trigger
- Company is in the right industry and size range
- No negative signals (not a customer, not in pipeline)

Timing score:
- 2+ strong signals: HIGH — prioritize, draft immediately
- 1 strong + 1 medium: HIGH — draft immediately
- 1 strong only: MEDIUM — draft, note in signals_used
- Medium signals only: MEDIUM — draft with ICP-level personalization
- Weak signals only: LOW — flag for human review before sending
- No signals: DO NOT DRAFT — flag reason = "no timing signals found"

### Layer 3: Targeting (who and when)
ICP fit + timing urgency together.
This layer catches accounts that look good on paper but should be skipped.

Skip the account entirely if ANY of these are true:
- They are an existing LeanData customer (caught in SFDC check)
- They have an open opportunity in Salesforce (actively being worked)
- They closed a competitor deal in the last 6 months
  (check LUCI and Avoma for competitor mentions — if Chili Piper or
  RingLead was recently selected, they just bought — do not contact)
- The target contact left the company
  (ZoomInfo contact data is stale if tenure end date is populated)
- No contact found matching ICP title parameters
  (do not default to a lower-quality contact — flag and skip)
- The company is too small to have a dedicated RevOps function
  (fintech: under 100 employees; insurance: under 50 employees)

Targeting validation output:
- targeting_valid: true | false
- skip_reason: string or null
- priority: "high" | "medium" | "low" | "skip"

### Layer 4: Content (the final mile)
Personalization only lands when layers 1-3 are already in place.
The writer agent is only invoked when layers 1-3 pass.

Content rules given the governing hierarchy:
- If priority = HIGH: use specific timing signal as opening observation.
  Do not use generic ICP hook — you have something better.
- If priority = MEDIUM: use the strongest available signal.
  ICP hook is acceptable if no company-specific signal exists.
- If priority = LOW: flag for human review.
  Do not send generic personalization — it wastes the contact.
- If priority = SKIP: do not draft, do not enroll.
  Log reason and move to next account.

The writer agent's output quality is directly proportional to the
quality of data the researcher surfaces in layers 1-3.
A great email on a SKIP account is still wasted.
A mediocre email on a HIGH-priority account still gets replies.
Prioritize data quality over email quality.
